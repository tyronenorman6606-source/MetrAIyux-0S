Proposal Standard

Specific beats generic.

A real staffing proposal should include role scope, headcount, location, schedule, pricing model, onboarding path, replacement policy, timeline, and next action.

Proposal Sections

What every serious staffing proposal should cover.

Hiring Problem

Summarize the employer’s pain: open roles, missed coverage, urgent project, seasonal pressure, turnover, or backlog.

Recommended Model

Temporary, temp-to-hire, direct placement, project crew, government support, or mixed staffing model.

Role Scope

Duties, location, shift, headcount, pay range, start date, required skills, screening needs, and disqualifiers.

Delivery Workflow

Intake, sourcing, screening, submission, interview or start process, onboarding coordination, and follow-up cadence.

Commercial Terms

Bill-rate model, direct-hire fee, conversion rules, payment terms, replacement policy, and signed agreement requirements.

Next Action

Approve the proposal, sign agreement, submit missing details, schedule kickoff, or confirm first candidate review window.
