Hair Care Guide

What should you know before color, relaxers, wigs, or extensions?

Major hair changes should begin with a conversation about condition, maintenance, and expectations. Color, relaxers, wigs, and extensions can all look excellent, but each choice changes the way the hair must be cared for afterward.

Color requires maintenance

Lightening the hair changes its structure. Even beautiful color can leave the hair more vulnerable if the client does not adjust the routine. Moisture, protein balance, gentler heat use, color-safe cleansing, and periodic trims become more important after a color service. The brighter or lighter the goal, the more maintenance should be expected.

Relaxers are chemistry, not convenience

A relaxer permanently changes the curl pattern. The service should be timed carefully, applied professionally, and maintained with conditioning and trims. Overlapping relaxer onto previously processed hair is one of the fastest ways to create weakness. Healthy relaxed hair is possible, but it requires discipline.

Wigs and extensions still require hair care

A wig or extension install should not mean the natural hair disappears from the plan. The scalp still needs attention, and the hair underneath needs moisture, safe tension, and takedown timing. Installs that are too tight or kept too long can cause breakage, matting, thinning, or scalp stress.

Ask better questions before booking

Useful questions include: What condition is my hair in now? What maintenance will this require at home? How often should I return? Will this affect my ends, edges, or curl pattern? Is my goal possible in one session, or should it be staged? These questions help prevent regret and create better results.

The best result is both visual and practical

A strong salon result should look good when the client leaves and still make sense weeks later. The right service plan considers lifestyle, budget, maintenance time, scalp comfort, and the health of the hair underneath the style.
