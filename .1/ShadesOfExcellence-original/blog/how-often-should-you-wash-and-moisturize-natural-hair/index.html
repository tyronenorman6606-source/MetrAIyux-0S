Hair Care Guide

How often should you wash and moisturize natural hair?

The honest answer is that natural hair should be washed often enough to keep the scalp clean and moisturized often enough to keep the fiber flexible. That sounds simple, but the correct schedule changes based on scalp oil, product use, porosity, density, sweat, and the style currently installed.

Start with the scalp, not the calendar

A clean scalp matters because hair grows from the follicle, and heavy buildup can make the scalp feel itchy, coated, or irritated. Many clients do well with a wash every 7 to 14 days. Clients who sweat heavily, use gels often, or have a flaky or oily scalp may need cleansing closer to weekly. Clients in low-manipulation styles may stretch longer, but stretching should not mean ignoring the scalp.

Moisture is not just oil

Oil can reduce moisture loss, but it does not replace water. A better routine usually starts with hydration from water or a water-based leave-in, then a cream or light oil to slow evaporation. Hair that feels dry hours after moisturizing may need smaller product layers, better detangling, or a trim if the ends are too worn to retain moisture.

Porosity changes the routine

Low-porosity hair often resists heavy products and can feel coated quickly. Warm water, lightweight leave-ins, and careful product amounts usually work better. High-porosity hair can absorb moisture fast but lose it fast, so it may need richer conditioners, protein balance, and stronger sealing methods.

A useful baseline

For many natural hair clients, a realistic baseline is cleansing every 7 to 14 days, deep conditioning as needed, moisturizing two to four times per week, and reassessing whenever the hair feels brittle, coated, limp, or itchy. The goal is not to follow a rigid internet rule. The goal is to keep the scalp comfortable and the hair elastic.
