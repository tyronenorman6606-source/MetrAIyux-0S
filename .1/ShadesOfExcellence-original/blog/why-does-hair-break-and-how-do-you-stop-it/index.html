Hair Care Guide

Why does hair break, and how do you stop it?

Hair breakage is usually not one single event. It is the result of repeated stress on the hair shaft until the strand can no longer stretch, bend, or recover. The most common causes are dryness, rough handling, excessive tension, chemical overprocessing, heat damage, and ends that have passed the point of repair.

Breakage versus shedding

Shedding comes from the root and usually has a small white bulb at one end. Breakage happens along the strand and often leaves short pieces in the sink, brush, collar, or hands. Knowing the difference matters because shedding and breakage have different solutions.

Dryness makes hair less flexible

Dry hair snaps more easily because it has less elasticity. This is why breakage often shows up near the ends, around the hairline, or in areas that rub against clothing. A routine built around gentle cleansing, conditioning, leave-in moisture, and regular trims can reduce the constant cycle of split ends traveling upward.

Tension damage is preventable

Tight ponytails, braids, wigs, installs, and edge control habits can cause breakage around the hairline and crown. Protective styles should protect the hair, not punish it. The style should not create headaches, bumps, soreness, or visible pulling at the scalp. If a style requires discomfort to look clean, the cost is often paid later.

Chemical and heat decisions stack up

Color, relaxers, silk presses, and repeated hot-tool styling can all be done well, but they require planning. Problems happen when services overlap too aggressively or when the hair is not strong enough for the next process. A strand test, realistic expectations, and maintenance appointments are more valuable than forcing a look in one session.

How to stop breakage

Reduce manipulation, detangle with slip, avoid tight styles, trim damaged ends, lower direct heat, protect hair at night, and build a routine that balances moisture and strength. The best plan is specific to the person. Hair density, texture, color history, scalp condition, and lifestyle all affect what should happen next.
