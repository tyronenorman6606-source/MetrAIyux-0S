# Client Delivery Standard - Bob Model

This is the simple version of what the Bob package means.

Bob's Smoke Shop is the example. The same model should become the normal customer package for any business we walk into.

## The Basic Idea

We are not just offering a website.

We are walking in with a business package already started for the client:

- a live landing/app experience for their business
- a QR flyer they can scan right away
- a free review page that explains the offer
- a team workspace starter lane
- a SkyEmail starter account
- a simple CRM/workflow lane
- a backup posture for their business data
- founder-managed hosting on SkyeNet

The pitch is simple:

> I already built the first version for you. You can review it for free. If you like it, we can turn it on, edit it, grow it, or upgrade it. If you do not like it, I can take it down.

## What Bob Was Going To Get

Bob's package should include:

- A live Bob's Smoke Shop app hosted on SkyeNet.
- A workspace preview so Bob can see what his business command area can become.
- A public flyer with QR codes.
- One QR code for the full offer/review page.
- One QR code for the actual live Bob app.
- A reserved SkyEmail account for the business.
- A clear note that the SkyEmail handle can be changed before he accepts it.
- A ConnectLog/Relay13 starter room for team chat and workspace messages.
- An AE-FlowPro starter CRM handoff after Bob claims the workspace.
- A CitadelDB backup posture after Bob claims the workspace.
- A free starter limit so the offer is valuable but not unlimited.
- Founder-only source custody, meaning Bob gets the hosted app for free but does not get the full source code unless we approve or sell a source handoff.

## What Every Client Should Get

Every client should get the same kind of package, adjusted for their business:

- Their business name, logo or working mark, contact details, and real offer on the page.
- A client-facing landing/app experience that looks like it was built for them.
- A QR flyer they can leave with the owner or manager.
- A pitch/review page that explains what is included.
- A live app link they can open on mobile.
- A free starter workspace.
- A reserved SkyEmail account or mailbox request path.
- A starter team chat/workspace lane through ConnectLog/Relay13.
- A starter CRM/workflow lane through AE-FlowPro.
- A backup promise that is honest and tied to the free plan limit.
- A simple path to accept, edit, upgrade, or decline.

The client should feel like:

> Somebody actually built something for my business before asking me for money.

## What The Free Starter Package Means

The free package should be real, useful, and limited.

Good free limits can include:

- a live hosted app or landing experience
- a small number of team seats
- a small number of workspace commands
- a small number of scans or proof exports
- a reserved SkyEmail starter mailbox
- basic CRM setup
- basic backup posture
- basic support follow-up

The free package should not promise unlimited usage.

## What We Should Say Out Loud

Use simple client-facing language:

- "I built you a live preview of your business experience."
- "This is hosted on our SkyeNet system."
- "This is not a random WordPress or Netlify resell."
- "You can scan this QR code and review it on your phone."
- "You can accept the free starter account if you want it."
- "Your team can use the starter workspace with limits."
- "If you want changes, we can edit it."
- "If you do not want it, we can take it down."
- "If you want more power, we can upgrade the package."

## What We Should Not Say Until It Is Fully True

Do not overpromise.

- Do not say SkyEmail is a fully active external inbox until the provider activation is actually finished.
- Do not say Relay13 is full Slack replacement unless that plan is actually sold and provisioned.
- Do not say the client owns the source code on the free plan.
- Do not promise unlimited messages, unlimited seats, unlimited storage, or unlimited support.
- Do not promise legal, financial, tax, or guaranteed sales results.
- Do not expose founder-only links, source downloads, private receipts, or internal admin pages on flyers.

## Source Code Rule

The free client gets the hosted app.

The founder keeps the source code unless one of these happens:

- the client buys the source
- the founder approves a source transfer
- the deal includes source ownership from the start

This keeps the free offer strong without giving away the whole factory.

## Flyer Rule

Every customer flyer should have two clear QR paths:

- QR 1: the offer/review page that explains what they are getting
- QR 2: the live app or landing experience built for them

The flyer should be written like a business owner will read it, not like a developer wrote it.

## Standard Client Flow

1. Find a real business.
2. Build the first version of their live app or landing experience.
3. Create the offer/review page.
4. Create the flyer with two QR codes.
5. Reserve or prepare the SkyEmail starter path.
6. Prepare the ConnectLog/Relay13 workspace lane.
7. Prepare the AE-FlowPro starter CRM lane.
8. Prepare the CitadelDB backup posture.
9. Save the client record in Founder Command.
10. Walk in, leave the flyer, and give them a simple way to accept or request changes.

## Upgrade Path

The upgrade should feel natural, not pushy.

A client can upgrade for:

- more seats
- more email capacity
- more workspace usage
- more pages
- a custom domain
- managed updates
- ads and local visibility
- CRM automation
- stronger backups
- backend functions on approved plans
- source code handoff
- secure source transfer through SkyeDrive, SkyeVault, instant link, or secure `.skye` pack

## One-Line Standard

Every client should receive a real business starter package: live app, QR flyer, review page, starter workspace, starter email path, starter CRM path, backup posture, clear limits, and a clean upgrade lane.
