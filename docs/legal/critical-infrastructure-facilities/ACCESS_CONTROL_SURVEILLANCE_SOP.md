# Access Control, Surveillance, Maintenance Entry, and Incident SOP

Effective date: May 29, 2026
Applies to: Arizona, Texas, Colorado, Illinois company offices and critical infrastructure suites
Status: Internal operating procedure for counsel/security review

## 1. Policy Statement

Company facilities that support engineering, server, database, AI, infrastructure, NOC, or executive operations are private appointment-only locations. Access is limited to authorized personnel and approved visitors. Maintenance, landlord, property-manager, cleaning, vendor, and inspection access must be scheduled, documented, and escorted except for a documented emergency.

## 2. Access Roster Procedure

The facilities/security owner maintains a live access roster with:

- Full legal name.
- Role.
- Employer.
- Access areas.
- Access hours.
- Badge/key/device number.
- Approving executive.
- Start date.
- End date or review date.
- Training/acknowledgment status.
- Emergency contact if appropriate.

Roster review cadence:

- Class A office: quarterly.
- Class B critical operations suite: monthly.
- Class C server/data room: monthly and after every personnel change.

Offboarding:

- Revoke badge/key/code access immediately.
- Recover keys/cards/devices.
- Rotate shared fallback codes if any existed.
- Document revocation time.
- Review recent access logs if the departure creates security risk.

## 3. Visitor and Maintenance Entry Procedure

Before entry:

1. Create work order or visitor appointment.
2. Confirm business reason.
3. Confirm sponsor.
4. Confirm areas to be accessed.
5. Confirm whether tools, ladders, carts, liquids, chemicals, cameras, phones, or vendor devices are needed.
6. Confirm camera notice and site rules.

At entry:

1. Check ID.
2. Visitor signs log.
3. Sponsor confirms approved areas.
4. Escort remains present.
5. Visitor may only perform approved work.

At exit:

1. Visitor signs out.
2. Escort confirms all tools removed.
3. Escort confirms no company equipment was touched unless approved.
4. Escort records notes, issues, and follow-up.
5. Facilities/security owner files log with work order.

## 4. Camera Operation Procedure

Camera setup:

- Use visible cameras.
- Post notice at suite entry and other appropriate points.
- Disable audio.
- Aim cameras at access points, doors, equipment zones, and operational corridors.
- Do not aim into private areas or neighboring tenant spaces.
- Test date/time stamps.
- Lock camera admin access behind MFA where possible.

Footage access:

- Do not browse footage casually.
- Access footage only for security, incident, legal, operational continuity, investigation, or audit reasons.
- Record who accessed footage and why.
- Export footage only for incident, counsel, insurance, law enforcement, or approved operational needs.

Retention:

- Normal retention: 90 days where storage permits.
- Class C retention target: 180 days where storage permits.
- Incident/legal hold: preserve until legal/security release.

## 5. No-Audio Rule

Audio recording is disabled by default across Arizona, Texas, Colorado, and Illinois sites.

No one may enable camera microphones, intercom recording, continuous room audio, hidden microphones, call recording, or meeting recording inside a facility without:

1. Written legal approval for the state.
2. Updated signage/notice.
3. Employee/contractor acknowledgment if required.
4. Visitor/vendor consent procedure if required.
5. System configuration record.

Illinois requires special caution because private conversations carry strict consent risk.

## 6. Biometric Access Rule

Do not deploy fingerprint, face geometry, hand geometry, retina, iris, or voiceprint access systems without legal approval.

Illinois deployment requires BIPA review before use, including written notice, purpose/term disclosure, written release, public retention/destruction policy, disclosure limits, and security safeguards.

Preferred access controls:

- Badge/card.
- PIN with named user.
- Mobile credential with named user.
- Physical key with custody log.
- MFA-backed access system.

## 7. Emergency Entry Handling

When the landlord/property manager enters during an emergency:

1. Capture notice time.
2. Request written emergency-entry report.
3. Preserve camera footage.
4. Preserve access logs.
5. Inspect doors, locks, racks, cables, equipment, sensors, UPS, cooling, and network state.
6. Compare equipment to asset inventory.
7. Open incident ticket if anything is abnormal.
8. Notify executive owner and counsel if risk is material.

## 8. Incident Evidence Hold

Evidence to preserve:

- Camera footage.
- Access logs.
- Badge logs.
- Key logs.
- Visitor logs.
- Alarm logs.
- Work orders.
- Landlord notices.
- Vendor communications.
- Emails/texts/calls about the entry or work.
- Photos of doors, racks, seals, cables, equipment, and room state.
- Server, database, network, cloud, deployment, and application logs.
- Backup state and restore logs.
- Asset inventory.

Evidence owner:

- Facilities/security owner starts the hold.
- Legal/counsel approves release.

## 9. Missing Equipment Procedure

1. Do not move remaining equipment until photographed unless safety requires it.
2. Preserve footage and access logs.
3. Identify last known good inventory.
4. Pull entry logs for prior 30 days, or longer if needed.
5. Identify all entrants with physical access.
6. Identify all maintenance/vendor work windows.
7. Interview sponsor and recent authorized personnel.
8. Notify landlord/property manager and demand preservation of building records.
9. File police report if theft is suspected and counsel/executive owner approves.
10. Notify cyber/security owner if missing equipment may contain data, credentials, keys, customer data, source code, backups, or models.

## 10. Public Notice Text

NOTICE: PRIVATE APPOINTMENT-ONLY OPERATIONS SUITE

Video surveillance may be in use for security, access-control, incident investigation, and asset-protection purposes. Audio recording is not used unless separately posted and authorized. Entry is restricted to authorized company personnel and approved visitors. Maintenance, vendor, landlord, and property-manager access must be scheduled, logged, and escorted except for documented emergencies. Unauthorized entry, unauthorized photography, tampering, or access to company equipment is prohibited.

## 12. Monthly Review Checklist

- Access roster reviewed.
- Departed personnel removed.
- Keys/cards accounted for.
- Camera health checked.
- Camera retention checked.
- Camera audio disabled verified.
- Visitor logs filed.
- Maintenance tickets closed.
- Environmental sensors checked.
- UPS/power status checked.
- HVAC/cooling status checked.
- Incident holds reviewed.
- Asset inventory spot-checked.
- Landlord notices reviewed for upcoming work.
