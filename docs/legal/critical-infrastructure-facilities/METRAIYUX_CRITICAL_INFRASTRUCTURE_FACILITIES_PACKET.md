## Executive Answer

Yes, the company can require a landlord, property manager, building owner, broker, maintenance vendor, or facility contractor to sign company-protective terms. The correct vehicle is not a loose website "terms and conditions" page. The correct vehicle is a signed lease rider, addendum, exhibit, site-security protocol, NDA, and access-control acknowledgment incorporated into the lease.

The company cannot unilaterally force those terms after a lease is signed unless the lease already allows it or the landlord agrees to amend. The clean move is:

1. Make the critical-infrastructure addendum a condition of signing the lease.
2. Attach it as an exhibit to the lease.
3. State that it controls over conflicting landlord form language.
4. Require the landlord and property manager to bind their employees, contractors, cleaning crews, repair vendors, brokers, inspectors, and agents.
5. Keep an executed copy in the company legal vault before moving equipment into the suite.

## Facility Classification

The company should classify each location before signing:

| Class | Use | Minimum posture |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Class A: Engineering Office | Workstations, meetings, admin, engineering access | Appointment-only entry, cameras at suite entry, badge/key logs, visitor escort |
| Class B: Critical Operations Suite | NOC/admin office, database administration, AI operations, production command systems | 24/7 authorized operator access, restricted maintenance entry, cameras at entry and critical corridors, written entry logs, incident holds |
| Class C: Server/Data Room | Company servers, database hosts, AI compute, backups, network gear | Dedicated locked room/cage, lockable racks, power/cooling redundancy, environmental monitoring, access logs, escorted non-company entry, no unlogged landlord access |
| Class D: Production Data Center | High-availability production systems | Prefer true colocation/data-center facility with contractual uptime, redundant power/cooling/network, fire suppression, remote hands, SOC controls |

Default recommendation: use a real colocation/data-center facility for production servers when uptime is mission-critical. Use company offices for engineering, NOC, administration, staging, and controlled local equipment unless the office is built to Class C standards.

## Non-Negotiable Business Requirements

Every candidate office or suite must support:

1. 24/7/365 access for named engineers, executives, operators, and emergency responders authorized by the company.
2. Company-controlled access to the suite and any server/data room.
3. Permission to install cameras at the company suite entry, server-room doors, equipment areas, and internal operational corridors.
4. Permission to install electronic locks, badge readers, keypads, rack locks, door contacts, and environmental sensors.
5. No landlord, property manager, maintenance, vendor, cleaner, inspector, or broker free-reign access to the suite.
6. Written notice before non-emergency entry.
7. Emergency entry only for life-safety, active property damage, urgent utility risk, law enforcement, or legally required emergency access.
8. Immediate notice after emergency entry, including identity, time, reason, areas entered, work performed, and any observed damage or equipment movement.
9. Appointment-only vendor/maintenance access with company sponsor, sign-in/sign-out, escort, and documentation.
10. No photography, recording, copying, inspection, inventorying, touching, unplugging, moving, or servicing company technology except inside a written work order approved by the company.
11. No audio recording by company cameras unless separately approved by counsel for that site.
12. No cameras in restrooms, locker rooms, changing areas, wellness/private areas, or other places where people have a strong expectation of privacy.
13. Utility and building-work notices that protect uptime: power, HVAC, water, fire alarm, roof, network, riser, access-control, loading dock, and elevator interruptions.
14. Ability to add redundant internet/fiber, circuit diversity, UPS, generator path, cooling, and fire protection if production equipment will live onsite.
15. Clear insurance, indemnity, incident cooperation, and evidence-preservation duties.

## Acquisition Checklist

Use this checklist before touring, during LOI, before lease redlines, before move-in, and before installing equipment.

### Building and Lease Fit

- Commercial lease permits technology operations, server equipment, network gear, racks, and after-hours operation.
- Lease permits company-installed cameras, access-control hardware, signs, sensors, cabling, locks, and door contacts.
- Lease permits 24/7 access for named personnel without building staff approval each time.
- Lease does not allow broad landlord entry without reasonable notice except true emergency.
- Lease allows the company to restrict suite access to appointment-only operations.
- Lease includes confidentiality for landlord/property manager observations of company systems.
- Lease has clear remedies for utility interference, lockout, unauthorized entry, repeated access violations, or failure to notify.
- Lease has a signed critical-infrastructure/security addendum with order-of-precedence language.

### Physical Security

- Suite has a single primary entry or can be segmented into controlled areas.
- Server room can be separated by solid door, electronic lock, camera view, and rack locks.
- No shared janitorial closet, mechanical closet, telecom closet, or landlord utility panel requires frequent unescorted entry through the server room.
- Door hardware supports company-controlled keying or electronic access.
- Building logs after-hours entry.
- Loading dock or freight elevator procedures can be documented.
- Visitor reception can support sign-in/sign-out and appointment-only access.

### Power, Cooling, and Environment

- Electrical load supports expected racks, workstations, UPS, cooling, and growth.
- Dedicated circuits available where needed.
- UPS plan documented.
- Generator or backup-power path identified if uptime needs require it.
- HVAC supports 24/7 cooling and server heat load.
- Temperature, humidity, smoke, water leak, and power sensors can be installed.
- Fire detection and suppression approach documented.
- Water pipes, roof leaks, sprinkler heads, and HVAC condensate risks are reviewed before server placement.

### Network and Availability

- At least two internet/fiber options available for critical offices.
- Riser, conduit, demarc, roof, and ISP installation rights confirmed in writing.
- Static IP, business fiber, failover cellular/satellite, and out-of-band access options reviewed.
- Landlord cannot delay reasonable carrier access without written cause.

### Operational Readiness

- Company access roster created before move-in.
- Badge/key custody log created before move-in.
- Visitor/maintenance log created before move-in.
- Camera notice posted before cameras are active.
- Incident evidence-hold process activated before equipment arrives.
- Asset inventory created before equipment arrives.
- Rack map, serial numbers, photos, warranty records, and chain-of-custody records captured.

## Company Access Model

### Authorized Access Roles

- Executive owner: final approval for site security posture and emergency exceptions.
- Facilities/security owner: administers access roster, cameras, logs, vendors, badges, and incident holds.
- Engineering operator: may access the suite 24/7 for company systems, deployments, outages, maintenance, and incident response.
- Contracted operator: access only for scoped work, approved windows, and access levels.
- Vendor/maintenance visitor: access only by appointment or emergency, with sponsor and escort unless the executive owner documents an exception.
- Landlord/property manager: no free-reign suite access; entry only under the lease and signed addendum.

### Access Control Rules

1. No shared keys, shared access codes, shared badges, or undocumented spare keys.
2. Every person with access must be named on an access roster.
3. Access is least-privilege by area and time.
4. Terminated or offboarded personnel lose access immediately, ideally within two hours.
5. Access roster reviewed monthly for Class B/C locations and quarterly for Class A locations.
6. Server/data room access is limited to those who need direct infrastructure access.
7. Non-company entry requires a record: person, company, ID check, sponsor, reason, area, time in, time out, escort, and work order/ticket.
8. Any forced, emergency, or unplanned entry triggers an incident review.

## Camera and Monitoring Policy

Company policy is visible video-only monitoring by default.

Allowed camera locations:

- Exterior-facing suite entrance where legally and lease-permitted.
- Interior-facing suite entrance.
- Server-room/data-room doors.
- Rack corridors and equipment zones.
- NOC/operations corridors where no private activity is expected.
- Shipping/receiving, storage, and asset staging areas.

Prohibited camera locations:

- Restrooms.
- Locker rooms.
- Changing rooms.
- Wellness/private rooms.
- Any location intended for nudity, changing, medical privacy, or similar private activity.
- Any area where the company cannot provide lawful notice or lacks the right to record.

Default retention:

- 90 days for normal footage if storage allows.
- Minimum 180 days for Class C sites if storage allows.
- Legal hold until release for any incident, suspected unauthorized entry, missing equipment, outage, property dispute, safety event, or legal claim.

Access to footage:

- Executive owner.
- Facilities/security owner.
- Legal/counsel.
- Incident-response lead.
- Law enforcement or third parties only through counsel or documented authorization unless emergency circumstances require otherwise.

Audio:

- Disabled by default in Arizona, Texas, Colorado, and Illinois.
- Audio may not be enabled unless counsel approves site-specific language, signage, employee/vendor acknowledgment, and consent procedures.
- Illinois sites should be treated as no-audio locations unless counsel gives written approval.

## Maintenance and Vendor Entry SOP

### Standard Entry

1. Work request created with date, time, requesting party, business reason, area, expected work, tools, and vendor names.
2. Company sponsor approves the work window.
3. Vendor/maintenance personnel provide identification at entry.
4. Visitor signs in and acknowledges company suite rules.
5. Company escort remains present unless executive owner approves a written exception.
6. Visitor performs only approved work in approved areas.
7. Any need to touch, unplug, move, inspect, photograph, repair, or open company technology requires new written approval.
8. Visitor signs out.
9. Sponsor records completion notes, issues, photos if appropriate, and whether incident follow-up is needed.

### Emergency Entry

Emergency entry is limited to life-safety, fire, flood, active leak, active power hazard, smoke, structural danger, law enforcement, or similar urgent conditions. After emergency entry:

1. Landlord/property manager must notify company immediately.
2. Landlord/property manager must provide identities of all entrants.
3. Landlord/property manager must provide time in, time out, reason, areas accessed, actions taken, and any property moved or touched.
4. Company reviews access logs and video.
5. Company inspects assets, racks, doors, seals, backups, power, cooling, network, and logs.
6. Company starts an incident record if anything is damaged, missing, moved, opened, interrupted, powered off, or unexplained.

## Incident and Missing-Asset Procedure

Trigger this procedure when:

- Equipment is missing, moved, opened, damaged, powered down, disconnected, or altered.
- Door, rack, badge, key, alarm, camera, lock, or sensor logs show unexpected access.
- Landlord, vendor, maintenance, cleaner, or third party entered without expected approval.
- HVAC, power, water, network, or building work caused or may have caused system impact.
- There is a suspected data, credential, source-code, model, database, or customer-information exposure.

Immediate response:

1. Preserve camera footage.
2. Preserve access logs, badge logs, alarm logs, visitor logs, work orders, landlord notices, and communications.
3. Freeze relevant system logs, backups, deployment logs, shell history, database audit logs, and network logs.
4. Photograph doors, racks, seals, cable state, equipment state, serial numbers, and affected areas.
5. Compare current assets against inventory.
6. Interview company personnel with recent access.
7. Demand landlord/property manager incident report if their personnel or vendors may be involved.
8. Open legal hold if loss, unauthorized access, outage, or claim risk exists.
9. Notify counsel if personal information, customer data, regulated data, source code, credentials, or production systems may be involved.
10. Determine whether cyber/data-breach notification laws are triggered.

## State Addenda

This section is operational guidance, not legal advice. Use the strictest rule when people, calls, vendors, or systems cross state lines.

### Arizona Addendum

Recommended posture:

- Arizona is the primary baseline for company facilities.
- Use visible cameras with posted notice.
- Disable audio by default.
- Do not place cameras in private areas.
- Include camera and access-control rights in the lease addendum.
- Require notice and escort for maintenance entry.

Key Arizona legal checkpoints:

- A.R.S. 13-3005 addresses interception of wire, electronic, and oral communications and includes consent concepts for conversations and communications.
- A.R.S. 13-3019 addresses surreptitious photographing, videotaping, filming, digital recording, or viewing and includes a security-purpose posted-notice exception in certain locations.
- A.R.S. 18-552 requires investigation after security incidents involving certain computerized personal information and, after determining a breach occurred, generally requires notice within 45 days subject to statutory conditions.

### Texas Addendum

Recommended posture:

- Use visible video cameras with posted notice.
- Disable audio by default even though Texas is commonly treated as a one-party consent state for many recordings.
- Do not place cameras in restrooms, changing rooms, or any invasive/private context.
- Avoid biometric access systems unless Texas biometric notice/consent obligations are reviewed by counsel.
- Put landlord access restrictions in the lease addendum.

Key Texas legal checkpoints:

- Texas Penal Code Section 16.02 governs unlawful interception, use, or disclosure of wire, oral, or electronic communications.
- Texas Penal Code Section 21.15 governs invasive visual recording.
- Texas Business and Commerce Code Section 521.053 governs certain breach notification duties.
- Texas biometric rules should be reviewed before using fingerprint, hand geometry, face geometry, voiceprint, retina, or iris systems.

### Colorado Addendum

Recommended posture:

- Use visible video cameras with posted notice.
- Disable audio by default.
- Do not place cameras in private areas.
- Treat security footage, visitor logs, access logs, and badge data as personal data requiring responsible retention and access control.
- Put landlord access restrictions in the lease addendum.

Key Colorado legal checkpoints:

- C.R.S. 18-9-303 and 18-9-304 address wiretapping and eavesdropping.
- C.R.S. 18-7-801 addresses criminal invasion of privacy for observing or photographing intimate parts without consent where there is a reasonable expectation of privacy.
- C.R.S. 6-1-716 addresses security-breach notice requirements.

### Illinois Addendum

Recommended posture:

- Use visible video cameras with posted notice.
- Disable audio entirely unless Illinois counsel approves written all-party consent procedures.
- Do not place cameras in restrooms, locker rooms, changing rooms, hotel bedrooms, residences, intimate-area views, or private contexts.
- Avoid biometric access systems in Illinois unless BIPA compliance is fully implemented first.
- Put landlord access restrictions in the lease addendum and require vendor acknowledgments.

Key Illinois legal checkpoints:

- 720 ILCS 5/14-2 governs eavesdropping and consent for private conversations/private electronic communications.
- 720 ILCS 5/26-4 governs unauthorized video recording and live video transmission in sensitive/private contexts.
- 740 ILCS 14/15 requires private entities using biometric identifiers/information to maintain a public retention/destruction policy, provide written notice, state purpose and length of use, obtain written release, restrict disclosure, and protect biometric data.
- 740 ILCS 14/20 creates a private right of action and statutory damages framework.
