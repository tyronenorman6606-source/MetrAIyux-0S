## 1. Purpose

Tenant operates appointment-only technology, software, database, AI, engineering, network, administrative, and critical operations functions. Tenant may store, operate, administer, or access computers, servers, databases, backups, network equipment, source-code systems, AI systems, customer systems, confidential business records, and security-sensitive technology at or through the Premises.

The parties agree that the Premises require controlled entry, documented maintenance access, security monitoring, 24/7 authorized personnel access, incident preservation, and restrictions on unapproved access to Tenant technology.

## 2. Order of Precedence

If this Addendum conflicts with the Lease, this Addendum controls with respect to:

- Access to the Premises.
- Cameras and security systems.
- Locks, badges, keys, and access devices.
- Maintenance entry.
- Emergency entry.
- Confidentiality.
- Critical utilities.
- Incident documentation.
- Tenant technology, servers, databases, and equipment.

All other Lease terms remain in effect unless expressly modified.

## 3. Appointment-Only Suite

The Premises are appointment-only. No person may enter the Premises unless:

1. Authorized by Tenant on the access roster.
2. Scheduled and approved by Tenant.
3. Escorted by a Tenant-approved representative.
4. Responding to a documented emergency under this Addendum.
5. Required by law, court order, fire department, law enforcement, or government authority.

Landlord, Property Manager, maintenance personnel, vendors, cleaners, contractors, inspectors, brokers, prospective buyers, lenders, and agents have no free-reign access to the Premises.

## 4. Tenant 24/7 Access

Tenant's named employees, executives, contractors, engineers, operators, and emergency personnel may access the Premises 24 hours per day, 7 days per week, 365 days per year. Landlord and Property Manager may not block, delay, lock out, require case-by-case approval, or condition Tenant's authorized 24/7 access except for immediate life-safety restrictions or lawful orders.

## 5. Tenant Security Systems

Tenant may install, maintain, replace, and operate reasonable security and infrastructure-protection systems, including:

- Video cameras.
- Electronic locks.
- Badge readers.
- Keypads.
- Door contacts.
- Motion sensors.
- Rack locks.
- Tamper-evident controls.
- Environmental sensors.
- Network/fiber equipment.
- UPS and power monitoring.
- Security signage.

Tenant will install systems in a lawful manner and will not place cameras in restrooms, locker rooms, changing areas, or legally prohibited locations.

## 6. Video Monitoring

Landlord and Property Manager acknowledge that Tenant may use visible video monitoring at entrances, server/data rooms, equipment areas, storage areas, and operational corridors for security, access-control, incident investigation, asset protection, and legal preservation.

Tenant's default policy is no audio recording unless separately approved by counsel and posted/consented where required.

Landlord and Property Manager shall notify their employees, contractors, vendors, maintenance personnel, inspectors, cleaners, and agents that the Premises may be video monitored and that entry constitutes acknowledgment of the posted security notice.

## 7. Landlord Keys and Access Devices

Any landlord-held key, code, credential, access card, override device, or emergency access method for the Premises must be documented in writing.

Landlord and Property Manager shall not duplicate, share, lend, transfer, or disclose any Premises key, code, credential, or access method except as required for documented emergency access or as approved by Tenant in writing.

Tenant may rekey, replace, or supplement locks and access systems as reasonably needed for security, subject to emergency access requirements and applicable law.

## 8. Standard Maintenance Entry

Before any non-emergency maintenance, inspection, repair, cleaning, building work, landlord tour, vendor work, or property-management entry:

1. Landlord or Property Manager must give Tenant written notice at least [48/72] hours in advance, unless the Lease requires a longer notice period.
2. Notice must state the date, time window, purpose, personnel, vendor company, areas requested, and expected work.
3. Tenant must approve the access window in writing.
4. All entrants must sign in and sign out.
5. All entrants must be escorted unless Tenant gives written exception.
6. Entry is limited to the approved areas and approved purpose.
7. No entrant may touch, move, unplug, open, photograph, record, inspect, inventory, or access Tenant technology except under a written work order approved by Tenant.

## 9. Emergency Entry

Emergency entry is allowed only for immediate life-safety threats, fire, smoke, active flooding, active leak, structural danger, urgent utility hazard, law enforcement, lawful government access, or other urgent conditions requiring immediate entry to prevent injury or material property damage.

For emergency entry, Landlord or Property Manager must:

1. Attempt to notify Tenant before entry when practicable.
2. Notify Tenant immediately after entry if prior notice was not practicable.
3. Limit entry to the emergency purpose and affected areas.
4. Use reasonable efforts to avoid touching, moving, disconnecting, photographing, or accessing Tenant technology.
5. Provide a written emergency-entry report within 24 hours.

The report must include:

- Date and time of entry.
- Date and time of exit.
- Names, employers, and roles of all entrants.
- Reason for entry.
- Areas accessed.
- Actions taken.
- Equipment, doors, racks, cables, locks, or other property touched, moved, opened, disconnected, damaged, or observed.
- Photos or videos taken.
- Follow-up repairs required.

## 10. Confidentiality

Landlord, Property Manager, and their personnel may observe confidential Tenant information during building operations. Confidential information includes business plans, customer information, servers, databases, AI systems, source code, credentials, security systems, rack layouts, network designs, equipment lists, signage, operating procedures, and incident records.

Landlord and Property Manager shall protect such information, disclose it only to personnel with a need to know for building operations, and not use it for any purpose outside the Lease. This section survives expiration or termination of the Lease.

## 11. No Interference With Critical Systems

Landlord and Property Manager shall not knowingly interfere with Tenant's servers, databases, networks, power, cooling, access-control systems, security systems, cameras, sensors, internet circuits, or equipment.

Except in an emergency, Landlord and Property Manager shall provide advance written notice of planned work that may affect:

- Power.
- HVAC.
- Water.
- Fire alarm or suppression.
- Roof.
- Riser or telecom room.
- Internet/fiber access.
- Elevators or loading dock.
- Access-control systems.
- Building entry.
- Suite entry.
- Noise, dust, vibration, or construction near the Premises.

## 12. Utilities, HVAC, and Building Work

For any planned utility interruption or building work that may affect critical operations, Landlord or Property Manager shall provide at least [5] business days' notice when commercially practicable and shall coordinate a low-impact work window with Tenant.

For emergency utility work, Landlord or Property Manager shall notify Tenant as soon as practicable and provide status updates until service is restored.

## 13. Incident Cooperation

If Tenant identifies missing technology, unexplained access, damaged equipment, altered equipment state, utility interruption, suspected unauthorized entry, or a security incident, Landlord and Property Manager shall reasonably cooperate by preserving and providing available building records, including:

- Building access logs.
- Elevator logs.
- Visitor logs.
- Work orders.
- Vendor schedules.
- Camera footage controlled by Landlord or Property Manager.
- Maintenance notes.
- Emergency-entry reports.
- Personnel names and vendor company details.

## 14. Visitor and Vendor Acknowledgment

Landlord and Property Manager shall require their vendors, maintenance personnel, cleaners, inspectors, and agents who enter the Premises to comply with Tenant's site rules, including:

- Appointment-only entry.
- Sign-in/sign-out.
- Escort requirement.
- No unauthorized technology access.
- No unauthorized photography or recording.
- Camera notice.
- Confidentiality.
- Incident reporting.

## 15. Insurance and Risk Allocation

The parties shall maintain insurance required by the Lease. If Tenant operates servers, databases, AI systems, or critical equipment onsite, the parties shall review whether additional property, cyber, business interruption, equipment breakdown, technology E&O, or special coverage is required.

Tenant remains responsible for securing its own technology systems. Landlord remains responsible for obligations stated in the Lease and this Addendum.

## 16. Breach and Cure

Unauthorized entry, repeated undocumented entry, sharing keys/codes, interference with security systems, failure to provide emergency-entry reports, failure to preserve records after incident notice, or unauthorized access to Tenant technology is a material security concern.

Tenant may seek all remedies available under the Lease, this Addendum, law, or equity, including injunctive relief, incident costs, rekeying, investigation costs, and termination rights if negotiated and enforceable.
