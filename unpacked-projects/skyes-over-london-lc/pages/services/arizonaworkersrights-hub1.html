How this works (simple + ruthless) 🧠

Most cases don’t fail because “the worker was wrong.” They fail because evidence is thin or the complaint went to the wrong place. The play is: classify the problem → preserve proof → pick the right agency → file within deadlines.

Step 1 — Identify what kind of dispute it is

Wage problems (unpaid pay, minimum wage, sick time), safety hazards, discrimination/harassment, retaliation, injuries/workers’ comp, unemployment, or “I’m a contractor and they breached the contract.” The editorial gives a bigger picture of why this happens and how workers get trapped in “paper-thin proof.”

Common Issues (what it usually looks like)

These are high-frequency Arizona workplace problems, what to document, and where to start. For exact eligibility and current rules, use the official links in each card. The WageCenter app is built to help you track the “proof trail” these issues require.

Unpaid wages / late pay / final paycheck problems

Examples: missing hours, “training not paid,” deductions you didn’t authorize, bounced checks, or never receiving your final pay. Track dates precisely and keep your timeline clean.

Minimum wage + earned paid sick time (AZ)

Arizona’s Fair Wages and Healthy Families Act covers minimum wage and earned paid sick time for many workers, with specific exemptions. Local minimum wage rules can also exist (ex: Tucson, Flagstaff).

Overtime disputes (often federal)

Many overtime issues are governed by federal law (FLSA). Arizona’s state wage-claim process typically doesn’t include overtime claims — so the “right door” may be federal or civil court.

Track start/stop times Keep schedules Exemptions exist
U.S. DOL WHD (local offices)

“I’m 1099 but treated like an employee” (misclassification)

Misclassification can affect minimum wage, overtime, taxes, and benefits. If the business controls how you work (not just the result), you may have employee protections even if they call you a contractor.

Save contracts + texts Document control/training Keep invoices + payments
DOL misclassification (official) IRS Form SS-8 (official)

Unsafe workplace conditions (ADOSH)

Hazards can include unsafe equipment, missing PPE, dangerous heat conditions, untrained operation, exposed wiring, fall risks, and more. You can report hazards, and in many cases you can remain anonymous.

Retaliation / whistleblowing

Retaliation can include firing, hour cuts, demotion, threats, or punishment after you asserted rights (pay, safety, sick time, etc.). Different laws have different deadlines — some can be as short as 30 days.

Workplace injury (workers’ compensation)

If you’re hurt at work, workers’ comp has a process and time limits. Getting medical care and properly reporting can matter. If an employer is uninsured, Arizona has special processes.

Contractor nonpayment (breach of contract)

If you’re truly an independent contractor, unpaid invoices are often handled as contract disputes (demand letter → negotiation → small claims/civil court), unless you were misclassified as “1099” while functioning like an employee. Save the contract, scope, invoices, proof of delivery, and all communications.

Evidence Kit 🧾 (saves locally)

Check items as you gather them. This saves in your browser (localStorage). You can export your notes as text for an agency or attorney. Then use the WageCenter app if you want structured case tracking and a clean workflow.

Core proof (almost always helpful)

Write a timeline: date → event → who was present → what was said/done.
List names, phone numbers, emails (boss, HR, payroll, coworkers, witnesses).
Collect pay stubs, direct deposit records, timecards, schedules, shift logs.
Save texts/emails/DMs about work, pay, discipline, or complaints.
Screenshot policies: pay, overtime, sick time, attendance, safety rules.
Write witness notes: what they saw/heard and when.

Situational proof (pick what fits)

Photos/video of hazards, posted schedules, time clocks (only if safe/legal).
Medical records, work restrictions, incident reports (injury cases).
Examples of unequal treatment: “others got X, I got Y” (discrimination).
Contract/invoices/proof of delivery (contractor nonpayment).
Misclassification signals: control over your schedule, tools, training, exclusivity.
Before/after proof: schedule cuts, write-ups, demotion after complaint.

What this site is (and isn’t)
