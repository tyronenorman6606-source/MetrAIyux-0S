Tour sequence

A systematic walkthrough for people who need the whole picture.

This page is built for someone you send the system to cold. It moves them from the operating problem, into the architecture, into security and proof, then asks whether the full website is worth their time.

Skip to Fit Check Open Platform Map

Start with the operating drag.

Most founder-led teams do not fail because they lack tools. They lose control because sales, candidates, clients, approvals, proof, and founder decisions keep arriving through unstructured channels.

Separate the public story from the control system.

The public overview is for education and routing. The admin surface is for private commands, connector control, production setup, approvals, and operator-only records.

Route customers into their own workspace.

The full site contains customer signup, onboarding, plan selection, service choices, and workspace paths. Customer work should not enter the founder's main command layer raw.

Use brains as scopes.

The 16-brain model is a routing system. Command, sales, staffing, compliance, technology, QA, and security review each get their own lane so the system can explain why a task went somewhere.

Gate risky action.

0meg4kAI is the public-name security and QA brain. It reviews tenant boundary risk, customer-to-admin escalation, public claims, connector abuse, and work that needs human approval.

Keep proof close to the work.

Proof receipts, ledgers, audit files, smoke-test results, and readiness pages make the platform more than a claim. Buyers can see a culture of evidence without seeing private secrets.

End with fit, not pressure.

The questionnaire asks whether a company has enough complexity to justify MetrAIyux 0S. If it does, route them to the larger website. If it does not, they leave with a clearer map of what to fix first.
