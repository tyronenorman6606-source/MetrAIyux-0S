    End-to-end process

    From one artist picture to a fan-money surface.

    The SupaBoy-style build is not a template offer. It is the high-touch version of a repeatable platform path: asset intake, artist identity, release metadata, media world, storefront, paid access, proof, and payout review.

    01

    Artist intake

    Artist claims or receives a SkyeMusicNexus node with a gate session, artist ID, profile photo, links, rights notes, and payout paperwork status.

    02

    The artist page gets gallery, EPK, booking, media, fan CTA, drop modules, and the visual universe appropriate to that artist instead of generic content.

    03

    Release packaging

    Uploads become release objects with preview audio, private full-access routes, track metadata, cover/canvas notes, and proof receipts.

    04

    Nexus Shop

    Fans can buy singles, bundles, full downloads, merch, support/tip products, fan passes, show links, or booking/media requests from the artist page.

    05

    Each checkout carries hashed artist ID metadata, drop ID, SKU, fee policy, Stripe payment IDs, fulfillment route, and payout-batch state.

    06

    The company reviews captured sales, fees, refunds, disputes, rights holds, and paperwork before releasing artist payout batches every two weeks.

    Artist management lane

    Artists can sign for serious management without giving up their music.

    This lane is for exclusive management, campaign management, and growth support. It is not a hidden distribution deal, publishing assignment, royalty grab, or master ownership transfer.

    Management onboarding

    Artist signs the correct 0S paperwork, completes rights notes and payout profile, receives an Account Executive lane, and starts a biweekly meeting rhythm for content, drops, and business decisions.

    • SkyeNet site deployment, SkyePay checkout, proof receipts, blog/content base, and AE handoff.

    365-day content push

    The 0S can package a longform blog base, SkyeNet publishing lane, Nexus feature slots, Media Over London creative requests, and June 2026 social-surface planning into a year-long publicity calendar.

    • Daily blog/post plan can be sold when scope, approvals, content review, and publishing access are written down.
    • Social expansion targets: Instagram, Facebook, X/Twitter, TikTok, Reddit, and LinkedIn content packs.
    • Claims stay tied to what is proven: content production and publication systems, not guaranteed virality.

    Ownership boundary

    Skyes Over London does not own the artist's music by default, does not become the distributor by default, and does not take royalties unless a separate signed agreement creates that right.

    • Artists retain masters, compositions, likeness, and media rights unless a signed agreement says otherwise.
    • Platform hosting, preview, gallery, and marketing rights are limited service licenses.
    • Distribution, licensing, sync, publishing, or royalty lanes require separate provider/legal proof before public claims.

    Drop pricing lab

    Let the artist choose the price. Respect the 13% platform fee.

    For small music purchases, Stripe's fixed fee matters. A $1.99 single is allowed, but the system should show the artist the real math before they pick buyer-covered or artist-absorbed fees.

    Buyer covers both is the clean default for low-ticket drops: the fan pays the artist price, platform fee, and estimated processing cost so the artist target stays intact.

    $1.99 singles

    Good for impulse support, but the 30 cent card fee is heavy. Use buyer-covered fees or bundle with tips/fan-pass upsells.

    $4.99-$6.99 packs

    Better for a single plus bonus file, alternate mix, short visualizer, lyrics PDF, or early access package.

    $9.99+ bundles

    Best for EP/album packages, private downloads, behind-the-scenes media, and campaign-level fan value.

    Vendor vs contractor lanes

    Use the right paperwork for what the artist is doing.

    This is operational guidance, not legal advice. The point is to route the artist through the correct 0S paperwork path before money starts moving.

    Artist as vendor / seller

    Use this when the artist is selling their own music, merch, support products, fan passes, downloads, or booking/media offers through the Nexus Shop.

    • Vendor/seller agreement with payout, refund, dispute, rights, takedown, and platform-fee rules.
    • W-9 or business tax profile before payout release.
    • Rights attestation for every paid preview/full-access drop.
    • Stripe Connect account or approved payout profile before automated transfer claims.

    Artist as independent contractor

    Use this when Skyes Over London is paying the artist for services: founding-core promotion, testing, content, campaign work, appearances, consulting, or deliverables.

    • Independent contractor agreement plus scope of work.
    • W-9 retained in company files and 1099-NEC reporting when required.
    • Clear deliverables, approval, invoice/milestone, and no-employee-benefit language.
    • Classification review using control, financial control, and relationship factors.

    Many artists should complete both

    An artist can sell through the platform and also do contractor work for the company. Keep the lanes separate: vendor payout for fan revenue, contractor payout for company-requested services.

    Do not casually call this payroll unless they are actually employees. For Nexus artists, use payout roster, vendor payouts, contractor payouts, or artist settlement.
