3. Why the base case is above the floor.

Replacement cost is what it costs to rebuild it. Deployed asset value is what you're actually buying. The difference between $2.35M and the $3.2M–$4.8M base accounts for four things the replacement number does not price in:

  • Multi-product architecture: this is not one product with 41 features. It is a platform with individually priceable standalone products — NorthStar/FS27 at $1,250/mo + $12,500 onboarding, kAIxU CodeStudio at $249–$1,890/mo, Agentic Growth Layer at $497–$2,997/mo, SkyeMusicNexus at its own tiered pricing, SovereignDocs at $297 per formation, Automated Content Engine at $67/mo. Each sells without the others.
  • Proof culture as a moat: 164 npm scripts, 36 proof scripts, 8 smoke/stress scripts, 4× repeated stress passes on SkyeMusicNexus alone. Rebuilding the platform costs $2.35M. Rebuilding the proof discipline alongside it costs significantly more and takes longer.
  • Zero time-to-first-customer: Stripe is live, auth is live, AI metering is live, email provisioning is live. A customer signs today, a workspace exists today. No infrastructure build step. That eliminates the deployment risk discount that applies to every other prerevenue asset at this price point.
  • SkyeMail is provisioning-ready: not API-partial. zoho-provider-smoke.json confirms provisioning_ready: true, account_id_discovered, default_from_discovered, organization_id_discovered. The email lane is production-ready at the provider layer.

6. Devil's advocate — the real remaining risks.

  • Zero ARR: still the hardest gap to close in any conversation. Every number above is capacity and potential, not observed revenue. A market buyer discounts meaningfully for this regardless of proof culture.
  • SkyeNet Functions isolated runtime: still in controlled preview. Arbitrary customer code execution is the enterprise unlock, not today's product. The deploy lane and static drops are proven; the function runtime is not.
  • Founder dependency: one operator built and knows this entire system. The IP is in the repo. The operating context is not yet documented for a second operator to run it. Real diligence flag for any buyer, even with receipts everywhere.
  • Scope legibility for a non-technical buyer: 41 priced surfaces across 4 registry groups is depth and a navigation problem simultaneously. A one-page ICP → product → price map is the fastest thing to fix before any serious conversation.
  • Contractor payout execution: approval-gated by design and correct. The lane collects, notifies, and approves. It does not yet execute external transfers. Worth separating clearly in any deal conversation.
